1 · Recommendation

✔ PROCEED WITH CONDITIONS — hold $33–41MM first lien (2.0–2.5× the corrected post-renewal base) beneath sponsor equity at a $125–150MM EV; the top of the band conditional on the HALCYON 2027–28 tour option being exercised, or the licensing base independently evidencing ≥$8.5MM of standalone EBITDA)

This is a decline on the leverage, not on the asset.

The binding constraint is dated, not modelled: FY25 EBITDA was made by a 62-date stadium tour that closes November 2026 — before the working close. The post-cycle year (FY27, no anchor tour in either plan) reads ~$9MM on the corrected basis, which caps standing debt at ~$45MM; the marketed $85–100MM staple frame fails that test at every sweep speed, and cash interest at the staple frame consumes ~48% of corrected EBITDA in the trough year before a dollar of inventory build. A $37MM facility with a 100% sweep clears every period; nothing larger than $41MM does. At this leverage the deal is an equity underwriting, not a credit one — and the equity floor is thin by construction: the company licenses its rights in from the artists, owns no catalog IP, and seven of the top-ten agreements carry change-of-control consent. Commercial reality, stated plainly: Aquarian may well lose the auction at $125–150MM — on this arithmetic, that is the correct outcome.

Four blocking gaps are absolute conditions: #11 · #27 · #44 · #58. If they are not answered, the recommendation is DECLINE at any price — not because the answers are presumed adverse, but because the Committee would be voting on an estimate. A flagged gap beats a guess. #31 (per-show settlement natives for the two European legs) stands at High — StageMetrix data is in hand and the seller's own files disclose the per-cap softening; the residual is promoter settlement detail.

Why — the recommendation reads this way

  • The band is sized to the weakest dated year, not the average year. The tour calendar is contractual and dated: when the anchor tour closes, FY27 supports roughly $45MM of standing debt and roughly a 9.0× EV on trough earnings. The bid must survive the year the calendar delivers first — not the blended average the CIM presents.
  • The renewal cluster is priced in, not assumed away. Nine of the top-ten agreements renew inside 30 months. The one renewal already signed re-priced the royalty +800bps. The base case carries a +540bps weighted step-up across the cluster — $(4.4)MM — and the bid still clears ~2.4× / ~19%. That is what "conditions, not hope" means.
  • Losing the deal at this price is an acceptable outcome. Winning at the marketed frame requires adopting a seller bridge this diligence rejects line by line, and underwriting a touring calendar no covenant can reschedule. A number that may not win, stated with its range, is exactly the information the Committee needs.

Q1 expanded — eight markers that the timing is the seller's, not the market's

  1. FY24 carries +$6.2MM of diligence adjustments; the most recent twelve months carry −$1.1MM. The apparent climb from $9MM to $26MM of adjusted EBITDA is adjustment asymmetry plus one stadium tour, not three years of operating improvement. [VERIFIED — databook rebuild]

  3. Per-caps crested mid-process. Per-attendee merch spend on the HALCYON legs peaked at $19.40 in March 2026 and has softened for eleven consecutive weeks; the CIM's per-cap page stops at February. [VERIFIED — settlement files vs CIM p22]
  4. The renewal was signed, and the CIM kept the old rate. HALCYON's March 2026 renewal (30% → 38% of net on D2C/retail) post-dates the QoE cutoff by three weeks and appears nowhere in the marketed number. The bank's model carries it; the CIM does not.
  5. A $5.1MM returns/markdown wave sits in FY26, the post-leg cleanup of tour-dated inventory, while FY25 books the sales. Post-tour markdown history runs 18–34% of remaining tour SKUs (§21).
  6. Drop revenue was pulled forward. Four HALCYON drops originally calendared for H1-26 shipped in Q4-25 — ~$3.8MM of revenue, disclosed in the drop calendar, never pro-forma'd out.
  7. The A&R signing pace fell during the process. New artist signings ran 14/yr (FY23–24) and 5 in the LTM; the pipeline file is dated October 2025. The roster the buyer inherits is the roster being harvested.
  8. The seller's own QoE grew between vintages. Between the February and April databooks, FY24 EBITDA gained +$0.8MM via a new "platform-fee dispute recovery" with no support tab in the earlier vintage. The move runs the seller's way, in the year that anchors the improvement narrative. [VERIFIED]

What is genuinely durable — stated fairly: the retail licensing book renews at 92% with escalators and zero adjustments in its history; the house brand ("Nightlark Standard") grew +18% on owned economics; D2C repeat rate is 31% and the email/SMS file (2.1M reachable) is a real asset; and the company's fulfillment record (99.2% on-time on drops) is why artists sign. That is a genuine platform case, and it should be argued at full strength before it is answered: it is ~$8–9MM of the EBITDA, not $24.8MM — and the price should be struck on it.

Q2 expanded — the number the seller does not show

The seller's headline metric is "~95% FCF conversion." Its own definition (CIM p38 n.4) — adjusted EBITDA − maintenance capex — is struck before the tour inventory build, before the post-tour returns wave, before the royalty true-ups and before a dollar of the 3PL/ERP migration. It is not an underwriting metric, and it is excluded from this analysis by standing decision. The same business, measured the way cash actually moves through a tour cycle, produces $(9)MM in the first trough year at the staple frame (full bridge at §5).

Q3 expanded — why the floor is thin exactly when it is needed

The merchandise rights are licensed from the artists; on the room's own paper, 7 of the top-10 agreements require artist consent on a change of control, and the HALCYON renewal adds a termination right on founder exit (§14). A distressed operator loses the next renewal cycle; the roster's mobility is highest exactly when the company's ability to pay advances is lowest — default probability and floor value are negatively correlated. The recovery build therefore counts only what survives that event: the contracted licensing tail net of consent risk, the house brand, net working capital, and the D2C customer file — $38–55MM — and the structure holds leverage inside it (§8).
